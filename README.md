# Key Finding

The trial balance is effectively balanced from **January through November**, with monthly differences ranging from **$1 to $8**, which are treated in the audit report as rounding differences.

December, however, is materially out of balance by:

> **$(97,403,376)**

The December difference closely corresponds to the year's net income of:

> **$97,403,378**

The audit reconciliation indicates that net income was moved into **Retained Earnings (account 29205)** while the revenue and expense accounts remained open.

As a result, the same net income is effectively represented twice in the December trial balance.

If the revenue and expense accounts were closed appropriately, the remaining difference would be only:

> **$2**

## The movement in Retained Earnings also matches the net income amount exactly, supporting the reconciliation identified in the report.

# December Reconciliation

The December imbalance can be reconciled as follows:

| Reconciliation Item                           |        Amount |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------: |
| Year-to-date net income                       |   $97,403,378 |
| Retained Earnings — November                  |  $220,435,578 |
| Retained Earnings — December                  |  $123,032,200 |
| Movement in Retained Earnings                 | $(97,403,378) |
| December imbalance as reported                | $(97,403,376) |
| Imbalance if revenue and expenses were closed |        **$2** |

The key observation is:

**Movement in Retained Earnings = $(97,403,378)**

while:

**Year-to-date net income = $97,403,378**

The two amounts match exactly in absolute value.

# Root Cause of the December Imbalance

The audit analysis identifies the apparent cause as the treatment of net income at year-end.

The sequence is:

1. Revenue and expense accounts accumulated balances during 2024.
2. The resulting net income was calculated as approximately **$97.4 million**.
3. Net income was posted to **Retained Earnings, account 29205**.
4. Revenue and expense accounts remained open.
5. Therefore, the net income effect remained in the revenue/expense accounts while also being reflected through Retained Earnings.
6. This produced the large December trial-balance difference.

In other words:

```text
Revenue + Expenses
       │
       ▼
  Net Income
       │
       ├──────────────► Retained Earnings
       │
       └──────────────► Revenue/Expense accounts remain open
                              │
                              ▼
                       Net income counted twice
```

If the revenue and expense accounts were closed to zero, the December difference would reduce from:

**$(97,403,376)**

to approximately:

**$2**

The audit report therefore identifies the year-end closing treatment as the principal explanation for the December imbalance.

# Audit Recommendations

The audit report identifies four primary follow-up actions.

## 1. Resolve the Retained Earnings / Net Income Treatment

Either:

* Close revenue and expense accounts to Retained Earnings, **or**
* Reverse the Retained Earnings entry.

The objective is to prevent December net income from being represented twice.

---

## 2. Obtain Supporting Documentation

Supporting documentation should be obtained for:

* The September debt reclassification.
* The **Prepaid acquisition (15160)** transaction that was cleared in December.

The unusual movement in these accounts should be supported by appropriate acquisition and accounting documentation.

---

## 3. Review Unusual Account Balances

Investigate:

* Debit-balance liabilities.
* Credit-balance assets.
* Accounts that disappear after November.
* Accounts that first appear in December.

The purpose is to determine whether these balances represent valid accounting activity, mispostings, or reclassifications.

# Important Data Notes

## Debit and Credit Sign Convention

The audit uses:

```text
Debit  = Positive
Credit = Negative
```

Therefore, negative balances in the tables do not automatically indicate an error; their accounting classification must also be considered.

## Cumulative Revenue and Expense Balances

Revenue and expense figures are cumulative during the year.

Consequently:

> **December represents the full-year cumulative revenue and expense balance.**

Monthly net income figures should therefore be interpreted as changes in cumulative balances rather than simply reading December as an isolated monthly income statement.

# Overall Audit Conclusion

The 2024 monthly trial balance is within small rounding differences from January through November. December contains a material imbalance of **$(97,403,376)**.

The reconciliation indicates that the December difference is associated with the treatment of the year's **$97,403,378 net income**. The corresponding movement in Retained Earnings matches the net income amount, while revenue and expense accounts remain open. If those revenue and expense accounts were closed, the remaining imbalance would be approximately **$2**.
In addition to the December imbalance, the audit identified **121 data-quality flags**, including unusual liability and asset balances, accounts appearing or disappearing around year-end, and significant acquisition/debt-related account movements.

The documented follow-up areas are:

1. Resolve the revenue/expense and Retained Earnings closing treatment.
2. Obtain documentation supporting the September debt reclassification.
3. Obtain documentation supporting the Prepaid acquisition activity.
4. Review unusual debit-balance liabilities.
5. Review unusual credit-balance assets.
6. Investigate accounts that first appear or disappear around year-end.
7. Confirm the source period of the workbook because the report notes that its figures are identical to an earlier template labeled 2022.
